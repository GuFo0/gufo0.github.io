5. Theoretical Discussion & Cybersecurity Interpretation

1. Comparison of Normalizations: Variance Behavior and Asymptotics

The behavior of the penetration score as the attack surface n grows depends entirely on the normalization chosen:

  • Total Score (Sn = ∑ Xi): Variance grows linearly: Var(Sn) = n σ². The trajectories diffuse outward over a parabolic cone with width ± √n. In security terms, the cumulative damage across n systems fluctuates wildly between different attackers; worst-case attackers achieve catastrophic breach depth as n increases.
  • Average Score (Sn / n — Law of Large Numbers): Variance decays inversely with system size: Var(Sn/n) = σ² / n → 0. By the Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN), for every ε > 0:
    limn→∞ P(|Sn/n − μ| ≥ ε) = 0.
    The trajectories collapse toward the horizontal mean line (μ = 0 for Rademacher; μ = p for Bernoulli). For an organization, this means the per-system breach vulnerability rate is strictly deterministic in the aggregate: individual random variations cancel out.
  • Normalized Score (Sn / √n — Central Limit Theorem): Dividing by √n scales the process exactly by its standard deviation. In the Rademacher model, E[Xi] = 0 and σ² = 1, which implies:
    E[Sn/√n] = 0,   Var(Sn/√n) = 1   ∀ n.
    The trajectories neither explode to infinity nor collapse to a point; they stay within a bounded standard normal strip. By the Central Limit Theorem, the distribution converges in law to 𝒩(0, 1).

2. The Rademacher Model vs. Bernoulli Model

• Rademacher (Xi ∈ {-1, +1}): Represents a balanced confrontation between security operations and attackers (e.g., +1 = compromise, −1 = detected and mitigated). Because μ = 0, the raw total Sn/√n is already centered and directly satisfies the classical CLT without subtracting a drift term.
• Bernoulli (Xi ∈ {0, 1}): Represents pure vulnerability exploitation (+1 = system compromised, 0 = defense held). Here μ = p > 0, meaning Sn possesses a linear deterministic drift n p. To observe standard normal convergence, one must center the variable: (Sn − n p) / √(n p (1−p)) → 𝒩(0, 1).

3. Cybersecurity Interpretation: System Size (n) vs. Attacker Landscape (m)

• System Size (n — Enterprise Scale): As an enterprise deploys more internal systems or defense layers (n ↑), the average breach risk per machine Sn/n becomes completely predictable (LLN). However, the cumulative intrusion score Sn experiences wider variance, demonstrating that defense-in-depth requires systemic controls: if individual success probability p > 0.5, cumulative breach is mathematically guaranteed.
• Attacker Population (m — Threat Landscape): When facing a large number of independent attackers (m ↑), the law of extreme values takes over. Even if the expected score is zero or negative (favorable to defenders), the tails of the Gaussian distribution ensure that with high probability, at least one attacker among m will achieve a statistically rare breach trajectory far into the positive tail (> +3σ).
